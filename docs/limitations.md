# Limitations

| Limitation | Detail |
|---|---|
| Presence | While TeamsRelay runs, Teams sees an active desktop: the page counts as visible and focused and the agent moves the mouse every minute, so the status stays *Available*. Busy, Do not disturb, meetings and calls set elsewhere still win. Teams mobile, set to notify only when you are not active on a desktop, may then stay silent: the push of TeamsRelay does not depend on it. While you click, type or scroll in Teams in the remote desktop (or in the window of the local relay), and for 3 minutes after, or after you open the remote desktop of the account from the app, the agent moves Teams nowhere: no chat switch, no mouse of its own, no Activity read; your own input keeps you Available. Just watching the desktop without touching it for more than 3 minutes lets it move Teams again. |
| Visible page | A visible page marks as read what arrives in the open chat. The agent keeps a chat open only while the app shows it (`viewing`), otherwise Teams stays on the self chat. Without a self chat in the list, the last chat opened stays open. The app counts as showing a chat only while it is on screen: 90 s after it goes to the background (another tab or window, a phone in the pocket) Teams leaves the chat, and what arrives there is not read. Back on screen after a minute or more, the app opens the chat in Teams again and shows the saved messages dimmed under *Opening in Teams* until Teams has it open, 2-4 s. |
| Expiring session | Conditional access invalidates the tokens and Teams stops syncing. TeamsRelay detects it (red status, a push after a minute signed out, another when Teams is back); the sign-in is repeated in the remote desktop, or in the window of the local relay. Often Teams' own **Sign in** is enough, with the session the browser still holds: 15 s into a sign-out the agent presses it once, and once more on Microsoft's page if it shows this account's tile or a lone *Sign in* or *Continue*; it never types, never acts on a page with a field to fill (password, code), never presses another account, never tries twice in a sign-out nor twice within 30 minutes, and never while you use Teams in the desktop or a call is on. Back within a minute of the press: no push. Still signed out: the push says the button did not help (about 75 s after the sign-out). These screens were never read on a real account yet: the agent logs their buttons, and presses nothing where they differ from what it expects. |
| Unconfirmed sends | A message counts as sent once Teams shows it with its status icon. When Teams does not within 15 s after Enter, the command ends unconfirmed: the message may be out, and the app says to check the chat before sending again. The web app shows it as not sent. |
| Repeated messages | The chat list shows the time of the last message for about a day, then its date; that change is not read as a new message. The same text sent twice within 150 s is notified once. |
| Notification of a group chat | A notification caught from Teams names a chat only when its title is a chat of the list. In a group chat the title may be the person who wrote: when that person also has a 1:1 chat, a tap opens the 1:1. The chat list detection names the right chat. |
| Chats only | 1:1 and group chats, up to 40 in the list, last 40 messages of the open chat. Team channels appear in the Activity feed but do not open. |
| Reading opens the chat | The agent reads the chat that is open in the remote Teams, so reading a chat from the app marks it as read in Teams. |
| Read by | Collected only for the open chat, on your last 5 messages. |
| Latency | New messages every 3-4 s, incoming calls within about 2 s, actions confirmed in 3-10 s, Activity feed every few minutes. |
| Language | Teams web must stay in English: some texts read by the agent (message status, feed titles, expired session) are English. The local relay gives Teams `en-US` as the language of the browser (`navigator.language`, `Accept-Language`, workers included) whatever the language of the machine; the menus of the browser itself stay in that language, and Teams may still write dates and times in a local format (`25/09`, `12:19`), which the agent reads as well. |
| Teams interface | When Microsoft changes the [selectors](teams-selectors.md), the affected functions stop working until they are updated. |
| Same display name | Chats are identified by the name shown in the list. With two chats of identical name, only the first one in the list is reachable. |
| Activity feed items | Reactions, mentions in channels and the other Activity items show in the Notifications tab, missed calls in the Calls tab too (red count), all in the unread counts of the account menu. The agent pushes chat messages; an Activity item reaches the phone only when Teams itself shows a notification for it, and a missed call when the check of an account checked every few hours finds it. Teams feed items carry no time: an older missed call that a longer read of the feed shows for the first time counts, and is pushed by a check, as new. |
| Incoming calls | A call reaches the phone only while the browser of its account runs: never for a stopped account, nor for one checked every few hours between its checks. The first push leaves within about 2 s of the call ringing on Teams web, then one every 5 s while it rings; Teams web rang only 5 to 9 s in the tests (voicemail or the caller ended it), so a phone that takes longer to wake may alert after the call ended. A call of an account of the browsers container can be answered from the app (see *Answered calls*); nothing declines a call. Missed, declined or answered elsewhere and taken by voicemail look the same: the notification says the call ended and how long it rang; the Notifications and Calls tabs list *Missed call from X* when Teams does (not for a call voicemail took). An account checked every few hours rings nowhere: its next check pushes each missed call its Activity feed lists, up to the interval of its checks late. Seen with 1:1 audio calls; video and group calls not yet. |
| Answered calls | **Answer**, on the banner of the app or on the notification in Chrome, takes a call of an account of the browsers container: its agent clicks *Accept with audio* in Teams at its next look, within about a second, and the app carries the sound both ways itself: the audio of the remote desktop of the account over its websocket, without its video. **Desktop** moves the call to the remote desktop, which then carries the sound; it opens instead of the sound in the app where the browser lacks WebCodecs or the remote desktop is on another site (`DESKTOP_URL`). The call is taken only while its toast shows: after the ring time of the account (30 s by default in Teams, less in the tests) it has gone to voicemail. The app asks the device for its microphone only while Teams records; the caller hears the owner once the browser has given it (permission asked the first time). A page the browser does not let play yet shows *Tap to hear*. The microphone and the speaker are the ones picked on the device (the sliders button of the banner, or Settings); a speaker can be picked where the browser allows it (Chrome on a computer), a phone plays where its system sends the sound. **Mute** presses the mute of Teams, so the others see the mute mark, and silences the microphone of the device at once, before Teams mutes about a second later; a mute pressed in Teams in the remote desktop, or by an organizer, shows on the banner within about two seconds and the device follows it, and the banner offers Mute also for a call whose sound is on the desktop. Where the agent cannot read the microphone button of Teams it presses nothing: the banner says *muted here only*, the device sends nothing and the others see no mute mark. Checked in real calls on 2026-09-29: the button read as Teams showed it, and the shortcut and the click of the agent muted and unmuted within a quarter of a second, the caller seeing the mute mark. Every sound of the desktop reaches the owner, a ring of another account included. A remote desktop open on another device when the call is answered is asked for its microphone instead of the phone: the server asks the page that connected first. Without earphones the loudspeaker can reach the microphone. While the call lasts the agent moves Teams nowhere on its own (no parking, no sweep of the chat list, no read of the feed). **Hang up** presses the end-call shortcut of Teams web (Ctrl+Shift+H) and waits for the microphone to stop (for a call muted in Teams that let the microphone go, for its microphone button to go); ending it in Teams in the desktop works as well. In the Android app, **Answer** on the ringing notification does the same and opens the account in its window, which plays the call (untested on a phone until the first live test). Not done: accounts on another computer (their sound is on that computer), declining, video and group calls, calls placed from the app. |
| Call sound | A web push cannot choose its sound: the device plays its notification sound, again at every push while the call rings. Android gives each site a notification channel of its own, whose sound can be changed (long-press a TeamsRelay notification, then its settings): it applies to messages too. iPhone: Safari there shows every push apart, ignoring the tag ([WebKit bug 258922](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=258922), open), and supports none of `silent`, `requireInteraction`, `renotify`, `vibrate`, action buttons: an iPhone gets one notification when the call starts and one when it ends, both with the default sound, and neither stays on screen. Windows keeps the notification on screen until closed. The open web app rings with a sound of its own while a call of any running account rings: in a browser tab, in the background too, once the page got a click or a key press since it loaded (the app says *Click to allow the call ring* until then); at once in the app installed from Chrome or Edge. Chrome's Memory Saver can put an idle tab to sleep, which then rings for nothing: keep the site active ([setup](setup.md#6-phone)). On a phone the web app rings only while it is on screen: Android and iOS freeze a web app in the background. The Android app of `mobile/` rings with the phone locked or the app closed: the ringtone of its Calls channel repeats until the call ends, the notification is tapped or the shade opened, 65 s at most; silent mode and Do Not Disturb apply, a TeamsRelay force-stopped in the Android settings gets nothing until opened again, and the battery savers of some phone makers can delay the messages. ntfy rings too, with its setting *Keep alerting for highest priority*, until the notification is swiped or opened; the end of the call replaces it with a quiet one (priority 2). |
| App icon count | The number on the icon of the installed app needs Chrome or Edge on Windows, macOS or ChromeOS, Safari on macOS 14 or later, or the Home Screen app of an iPhone (iOS 16.4 or later, notifications allowed); Chrome on Android shows none (its launcher shows the dot of the notifications). While no window of the app is on screen, a push puts a dot there instead of the number, until the app is back on screen. |
| Message sound | A web push cannot choose its sound either: the bell of new messages plays only while TeamsRelay is open, installed or in a tab (a tab after a click in it); with the app closed the device plays its notification sound, which Android lets change per site. |
| Offline phones | The push service keeps a notification for 24 hours; a device offline longer misses it (the chat list still shows the message). Back online, the phone shows one notification per chat with its last five messages. |
| Delivery | The push service confirms that it took a push, not that the device showed it. TeamsRelay sends again only what the push service refused for now (429, 5xx) or did not answer. |
| Bots without preview | New message detection relies on the preview: bots that show none can be missed. |
| Files | The app sends images only: one PNG, JPEG, GIF or WebP of up to 10 MB per message, pasted, dropped or attached, with the text as caption; not in a reply or an edit. Any other file (Excel, PDF...) is refused with a message. Attachments received show with their name; the download works only for SharePoint files the browser session reaches: behind Defender for Cloud Apps the link answers with a web page and the app says to open the file in Teams. Sending and saving other files is a manual step in Teams. |
| Tagging people | `@` in the app tags the people Teams lists for that chat (in a group chat its members, you excluded; in the self chat nobody), in a new message only: not in a reply, an edit or the caption of an image. The list of members is read from Teams on the first `@` of a chat and again after an hour. |
| AI clients (MCP) | Read only, one token acting as the administrator of `.env`, no OAuth: clients that connect only through OAuth (claude.ai connectors) cannot use it. `read_chat` gives the messages saved the last time the chat was open; `refresh_chat` opens it in Teams, which marks it read. Details: [mcp.md](mcp.md). |
| iPhone | Push notifications reach only the app installed on the Home Screen. |
| Android app | The Tauri app (`mobile/`) shows the web app in a WebView, which has no Web Push: its notifications come through Firebase, once the server has the service account key of its Firebase project and the APK was built with its `google-services.json` ([setup](setup.md#android-app)). Calls ring, with **Answer** for an account of the browsers container; each chat has one notification with its last lines, alerts have their own; no reply from the notification. A phone signed out of the web app in the app is forgotten at the next start of the app; one whose app was removed is forgotten at the next push. |
| One owner | Every Teams account of a server belongs to one person: all of them show their browser window on the one remote desktop. `SLOT_COUNT` caps their number (default 4). |
| One container | The browsers of every account run in one container: a new browsers image (Chromium update, agent release) restarts every account. |
| Checked accounts | An account checked every few hours runs only during its checks, one account at a time, so a check can start later than due. The app shows the chats and counts of the last check and takes no command (send, open, refresh), during a check either: sending needs Always on. The messages that arrived meanwhile are not pushed one by one (new message detection starts again at each start): one notification per check says how many chats are unread. A sign-in to do is found at the next check; to sign in, **Start to sign in** keeps the browser up ten minutes. No automatic check with its push at 8-11 and 17-20 on such an account. |
| Local relay: one account, text | The local relay serves one Teams account: chats, text from the phone, reply, reactions, edit, delete. No Activity feed, "Read by", @mentions, images to send or downloads. |
| Local relay: restarts | A restart of the browser keeps the Microsoft session only when the tenant lets it persist ("Stay signed in"): otherwise every restart asks for a new sign-in in the relay window. Messages that arrive while the relay is down are not pushed afterwards; they show in the chat list. |
| Local relay: reach | The phone needs HTTPS to the relay (service worker): Tailscale Serve, or the LAN with a certificate the phone trusts. The relay listens on loopback until the owner chooses. |
| Account on another computer | Its data on the server is what its relay last sent: while the relay or its computer is off, the account shows **Not connected**, nothing arrives, and commands answer 409 (a command queued just before the relay went away fails once it is two minutes old, when the relay is back). The sign-in happens on that computer, never in the remote desktop. Notifications go through the server, one at a time in the order they came: a server the relay cannot reach delivers none for that time, and a notification that waited its turn over two minutes is dropped. Rows and files the server would refuse (a field longer than it takes, an image whose bytes are not its type, past `RELAY_QUOTA_MB`) stay on that computer, logged by the relay: the app shows them missing. A file refused goes again when a later sync names it. |
| Account on another computer added on a slot freed in the same second | The commands of an account are numbered in a series named after the second the account took its slot. An account removed and another added on the same slot within that second share the series: a relay moved from the first to the second with its `relay.db` takes the first commands of the second for ones it already ran, and runs none of them. Rare by hand; wait a second, or start that relay on a new `STATE_DIR`. |
| Local relay: desktop | The relay browser is a window of the desktop session: the machine must stay on, awake and logged in; a Windows service cannot show it. |
